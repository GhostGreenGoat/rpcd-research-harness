# General weighted-level Schur recursion and the exact `W4` blocker

Date: 2026-08-22

Status: the recursion below is exact algebra (E3 proof draft); the `W4`
full-recovery statement is an open candidate with fixed exact E2 controls.
No claim in this note proves the all-depth RPCD conjecture.

## 1. General weighted Bellman step

Let `A` be a unit-diagonal SPD parent of size `m`, let
`0<mu<=lambda_min(A)`, and put `d=m-1`.  The exact weighted recursion is

```text
C_t(A)=I/(2m)+(1/m)sum_i L_i^T C_(t-1)(C_i)L_i.          (1.1)
```

Suppose a direction-preserving child lower state is available:

```text
C_(t-1)(C)>=P_(t-1)(C;mu)
            =alpha C^-1+Q(C;mu),
alpha=t mu/(2d),
Q(C;mu)>=0.                                               (1.2)
```

For the actual parent extension at coordinate `i`, write

```text
s_i=1-b_i^T C_i^-1b_i,
c_i=C_i^-1b_i,
D_i=(1/s_i)L_i^T c_i c_i^T L_i.                          (1.3)
```

Define the maximal recoverable Schur coefficient

```text
kappa_i=sup{k>=0: Q(C_i;mu)>=(k/s_i)c_i c_i^T}.          (1.4)
```

Equivalently, when `c_i` lies in the range of `Q_i`,

```text
kappa_i=s_i/[c_i^T Q_i^dagger c_i];                      (1.5)
```

it is zero if the range condition fails and `+infinity` if `c_i=0`.  Put
`r_i=(alpha-kappa_i)_+`.  The block inverse identity gives

```text
L_i^T P_(t-1)(C_i;mu)L_i
 >=alpha(A^-1-e_i e_i^T)-r_iD_i.                         (1.6)
```

Substitution into (1.1) yields

```text
C_t(A)>=alpha A^-1+qI-(1/m)sum_i r_iD_i,
q=[d-t mu]/(2md).                                        (1.7)
```

In the intended half-depth range `t<=ceil(m/2)`, `q>=0`.  Using
`I>=mu A^-1`, the remaining free surplus before the directional defects is

```text
alpha-(t+1)mu/(2m)+qmu
 =t mu(1-mu)/(2md).                                      (1.8)
```

Therefore the exact sufficient aggregate condition for `W_t` is

```text
(1/m)sum_i r_iD_i
 <=[t mu(1-mu)/(2md)]A^-1.                               (1.9)
```

The stronger termwise condition

```text
kappa_i>=alpha  for every i                              (1.10)
```

makes the left side zero and closes the weighted step immediately.  This is
the general recursive scalar/matrix condition generated by the abstract
Schur-envelope lemma.  It preserves each child direction; there is no Jensen
averaging of inverses.

## 2. Exact fourth-level state

The audited `L3` theorem candidate supplies, for every child of size `d>=3`,

```text
L3(C)>=(2mu/d)C^-1.                                      (2.1)
```

Define the exact next sufficient Bellman state

```text
L4(A;mu)=I/(2m)+(1/m)sum_i L_i^T L3(C_i)L_i.             (2.2)
```

Since `C3(C_i)>=L3(C_i)`, one has

```text
C4(A)>=L4(A;mu).                                         (2.3)
```

Here `L3` itself is independent of `mu`; the parameter is written in `L4`
only to emphasize the decomposition

```text
alpha=2mu/d,
Q_i=L3(C_i)-alpha C_i^-1.                                (2.4)
```

The sharp termwise `W4` lemma is now completely explicit:

```text
Q_i >=[2mu/(d s_i)]c_i c_i^T,   every i,                 (2.5)
```

for the relevant parent range `m>=7` (`4<=ceil(m/2)`).  If (2.5) holds, then

```text
L4(A)>=(5mu/(2m))A^-1,                                   (2.6)
```

and the residual scalar surplus is

```text
2mu(1-mu)/[m(m-1)] A^-1.                                 (2.7)
```

If termwise recovery fails, the weaker exact aggregate allowance from (1.9)
is

```text
(1/m)sum_i(2mu/d-kappa_i)_+D_i
 <=[2mu(1-mu)/(md)]A^-1.                                 (2.8)
```

Thus (2.5) and (2.8), rather than an unexpanded degree-six polynomial, are
the two falsifiable `W4` targets.

Woodbury gives a useful equivalent formulation.  With the outer Schur
complement

```text
S_i=C_i-b_i b_i^T,
S_i^-1=C_i^-1+(1/s_i)c_i c_i^T,                           (2.9)
```

condition (2.5) is exactly

```text
boxed: L3(C_i)>=(2mu/d)S_i^-1.                            (2.10)
```

Moreover, Schur complementation of `A-mu I` gives

```text
S_i>=mu I+[mu/(1-mu)]b_i b_i^T>=mu I                    (2.11)
```

for `mu<1`.  Thus the next lemma asks for stability of the `L3` certificate
under the rank-one reduction `C_i -> S_i`, whose diagonal is no longer one.
This is more precise than a generic degree-six matrix inequality.

There is an even sharper reduction if one asks for (2.5) uniformly over all
admissible one-coordinate extensions of a fixed child `C`.  Let

```text
Q(C)=L3(C)-(2mu/d)C^-1.                                  (2.12)
```

Parameterize `c=tv` with `v` in `range(C-mu I)`.  Saturating the Schur
constraint of `A-mu I` gives

```text
t^2 v^T C^2(C-mu I)^-1v=1-mu.                           (2.13)
```

The rank-one demand is increasing in `t^2`.  The rank-one inverse criterion
and inverse-order reversal show that uniform recovery is equivalent to

```text
Q(C)>=[2(1-mu)/d]
      (C-mu I)[C(C+(1-mu)I)]^-1.                         (2.14)
```

The right side is zero on the `mu` eigenspace, where the Schur range condition
forces the outer coupling to vanish.  Adding `(2mu/d)C^-1` to both sides
simplifies eigenvalue by eigenvalue:

```text
boxed: L3(C)>=(2/d)[C+(1-mu)I]^-1.                       (2.15)
```

Thus (2.15), for every unit-diagonal `C>=mu I` of size `d>=6`, is a single
explicit rational matrix inequality sufficient for the full termwise `W4`
step.  It is stronger than what one fixed parent extension needs, but it is
the cleanest falsifiable universal lemma.  The averaged fallback (2.8)
remains available if (2.15) is false.

## 3. Why the global spectral remainder alone cannot prove (2.5)

The audited `L3` certificate has the exact decomposition, for child dimension
`d>=4`,

```text
Q_i=(1/d)sum_j L_j^TM_jL_j
    +q_d(I-mu C_i^-1)+delta_d C_i^-1,                    (3.1)

q_d=(d-1-3mu)/[2d(d-1)],
delta_d=3mu(1-mu)/[2d(d-1)].                             (3.2)
```

Dropping the anisotropic first term gives the spectral proxy

```text
Q_spec(lambda)=[q_d(lambda-mu)+delta_d]/lambda.           (3.3)
```

The sharp Schur envelope for (2.5) would require

```text
(d-1-3mu)x+3mu(1-mu)
 >=4(d-1)(1-mu)x/(x+1),
x=lambda-mu.                                             (3.4)
```

At `mu->0`, (3.4) reduces to `x>=4x/(x+1)`, which fails for
`0<x<3`.  The failure is exact at a strictly positive feasible point:

```text
d=6, mu=1/100, lambda=1,
Q_spec(lambda)=33/400,
required envelope=3267/19900,
gap=-6501/79600.                                         (3.5)
```

A unit-diagonal realization is the size-seven direct sum of two
`[[1,99/100],[99/100,1]]` blocks and `I_3`.  The relevant child has an
eigenvalue one and floor `mu`.  Importantly, the **full** anisotropic `Q_i`
passes the rank-one test on this family with exact ratio `99/199<1`.

Hence the current blocker is not the global spectral part of `L3`; it is
proving that the retained sum of child Schur-envelope remainders `M_j` repairs
the exact deficit in (3.5).  Discarding those matrices repeats the forbidden
anisotropy loss.

## 4. Exact controls and route boundary

`scripts/iter6_w4_schur_recovery_exact.py` records fixed rational checks (not
a search).  The full termwise ratio is at most one on parent size seven for:

- positive signed-rank-one;
- regular simplex;
- positive-size-two plus simplex-size-five direct sum;
- two positive size-two blocks plus identity;
- an asymmetric rational rank-three Gram lift.

These are E2 controls only.  The same termwise statement is false if asserted
for every parent `m>=4`: positive equicorrelation at `m=4,mu=1/4` has exact
ratio `2304/1859>1`.  This does not refute (2.5), whose hierarchy-relevant
range starts at `m=7`, but it rules out a dimension-free base-case shortcut.

The smallest unresolved analytic statement is therefore (2.5) for child
dimension `d>=6`, or the averaged fallback (2.8).  Any proof must use the
anisotropic `M_j` term in (3.1); the remaining spectral terms are exactly
insufficient.

## 5. An analytic `W4` slice: isolated outer coupling

There is a nontrivial family on which (2.5) can be proved exactly.  Suppose

```text
C=[1] direct_sum B,
b=r e_1.                                                  (5.1)
```

Then the full parent is the direct sum of the correlation block
`[[1,r],[r,1]]` and `B`.  The parent floor implies

```text
mu<=1-|r|.                                                (5.2)
```

The child definition of `L3` gives an exact scalar value on the isolated
coordinate:

```text
e_1^T L3(C)e_1=2/d.                                       (5.3)
```

Indeed, deleting the isolated coordinate contributes zero there.  Each of
the other `d-1` deletions leaves an isolated unit coordinate in a child of
size `d-1`; its `P_(d-1)` eigenvalue is `3/[2(d-1)]`.  Hence
`1/(2d)+(d-1)*3/[2d(d-1)]=2/d`.

Both `L3(C)` and `C^-1` reduce the isolated coordinate, so the only nontrivial
rank-one comparison in (2.5) is

```text
2(1-mu)/d >=[2mu/d] r^2/(1-r^2),                         (5.4)
```

or `r^2<=1-mu`.  This follows from (5.2):
`r^2<=(1-mu)^2<=1-mu`.  The complementary `B` block is PSD by the audited
`L3` theorem.  Therefore the full termwise Schur recovery (2.5), and hence
the `W4` sufficient state (2.6), holds for every parent of the form (5.1) in
the hierarchy-relevant range.

This proves analytically the two-positive-block-plus-identity control whose
spectral-only proxy failed in (3.5).  The repair comes exactly from the
anisotropic child term, not from a better scalar bound.

## 6. Analytic positive-equicorrelation `W4` slice

The full termwise recovery can also be proved for every positive
equicorrelation parent in the relevant dimensions.  Let the child size be
`d>=6` and put

```text
C=mu I+(1-mu)11^T,
b=(1-mu)1,
Lambda=d-(d-1)mu.                                        (6.1)
```

The parallel eigenvalue of the exact `L3(C)` state simplifies to

```text
ell_parallel=(2mu^4+mu^2+1)/(2d).                        (6.2)
```

Moreover,

```text
s=mu(d+1-dmu)/Lambda,
||c||^2=d(1-mu)^2/Lambda^2.                              (6.3)
```

For `alpha=2mu/d`, the normalized rank-one demand is

```text
R=alpha ||c||^2/
  {s[ell_parallel-alpha/Lambda]}
 =4d(1-mu)/[(d+1-dmu)P_d(mu)],                           (6.4)
```

where

```text
P_d(mu)=2(d-1)mu^4-2mu^3+(d-3)mu^2-3mu+d.               (6.5)
```

Exact subtraction gives

```text
1-R=Lambda T_d(mu)/[(d+1-dmu)P_d(mu)],                  (6.6)

T_d(mu)=2dmu^4-2mu^3+(d-2)mu^2-3mu+d-3.                (6.7)
```

At `d=6`, the degree-four Bernstein coefficients on `0<=mu<=1` are

```text
P_6: [6, 21/4, 5, 19/4, 14],
T_6: [3, 9/4, 13/6, 9/4, 14].                            (6.8)
```

They are strictly positive.  For every `d>=6`, both polynomials add

```text
(d-6)(2mu^4+mu^2+1)>=0                                  (6.9)
```

to their `d=6` version.  Therefore `R<1` for `0<mu<1`, with the identity
endpoint handled directly.  This proves (2.5), and hence the `W4` sufficient
state, on the complete positive-equicorrelation family for every
hierarchy-relevant parent dimension `m=d+1>=7`.

In fact the stronger uniform-extension inequality (2.15) holds on this
family.  The parallel sector is exactly the comparison already proved above.
On the transverse sector, writing `a=1-mu`, exact subtraction gives

```text
ell_transverse-2/d
 =a U_d(a)/[2d(d-2)(d-1)].                               (6.10)
```

Here

```text
U_d(a)=2a^3d^2-6a^3d+4a^3-4a^2d^2+16a^2d-16a^2
       +3ad^2-17ad+26a+10d-20.                           (6.10a)
```

At `d=6`, the degree-four Bernstein coefficients of `aU_6(a)` are

```text
[0, 10, 76/3, 30, 48].                                  (6.11)
```

The derivative `partial_d U_d` at `d=6` has degree-three Bernstein
coefficients

```text
[10, 49/3, 12, 15],                                     (6.12)
```

and `partial_d^2 U_d=2a(2a^2-4a+3)>=0`.  Hence `U_d>=0`
for every `d>=6`.  This proves (2.15), not only the natural-parent direction,
on the complete positive family.

## 7. Analytic negative-equicorrelation `W4` slice

Let the parent off-diagonal be `a<0` and parameterize the SPD interval by

```text
z=1-mu=-da,       0<z<1.                                 (7.1)
```

The child has size `d`, parallel eigenvalue
`Lambda=[d(1-z)+z]/d`, and the same `L3` parallel formula as before.  Direct
exact simplification gives the normalized recovery demand

```text
R=4d^5 z/[(d+z)P_d(z)],                                  (7.2)
```

where

```text
P_d(z)=d^4(14-10z)+d^3z(23-13z)
      +d^2z^2(21-8z)+2dz^3(5-z)+2z^4.                   (7.3)
```

Every grouped factor in (7.3) is strictly positive on `0<=z<=1`.  Moreover,

```text
1-R=
 [d(1-z)+z]
 [14d^4+23d^3z+21d^2z^2+10dz^3+2z^4]
 /[(d+z)P_d(z)] >0.                                     (7.4)
```

Thus (2.5) also holds on the complete negative-equicorrelation family.  In
particular both signs, including their near-singular boundary limits, pass
the exact `W4` Schur recovery in every relevant dimension.

The uniform-extension strengthening (2.15) also holds.  Its parallel gap is
the manifestly positive numerator in (7.4).  The transverse gap has positive
denominator and numerator `z N_d(z)`.  Bounding only its negative monomials,

```text
21d^5+17d^4+17d^3+10d^2+2d <=5d^6,                     (7.5)
```

for `d>=6`, while the leading positive part is

```text
4d^7-8d^6=4(d-2)d^6>=16d^6.                             (7.6)
```

All omitted monomials are nonnegative on `0<=z<=1`, so the transverse
numerator is strictly positive.  Thus both spectral sectors satisfy (2.15).

matching: arbitrary unequal and signed `2 x 2` correlation blocks plus
isolated coordinates.  Here `H^2=D`, `F=H`, and `S=(d-2)D`; the two block
eigenlines reduce to exact positive scalar factorizations.  This theorem has
